The challenge

What your website has to do

People look for a lawyer when something important is at stake. They need to know quickly whether your firm handles their problem, who would work on it, and how to reach you. A law firm website that buries practice areas in long paragraphs, or hides attorneys behind a generic team page, loses that client to the next search result.

We build law firm websites around how clients actually search: a clear page for every practice area, a profile for every lawyer, and contact paths that feel personal. The design reflects the firm's standing, without the clutter.

Practice-area pages that rank

Most legal searches are specific: "estate planning lawyer Savannah" or "commercial lease solicitor Manchester". A firm that lists twenty practice areas on one page competes for none of them. A dedicated page for each area, with real substance and a clear next step, gives the firm a fair chance in every one.

Attorney profiles do more work than any other page

For many firms, individual lawyer profiles are among the most visited pages on the site. Clients often have a name from a referral and want to check that person before they call. A good profile shows the lawyer's practice areas, experience, education, admissions and a direct way to get in touch, with a professional photo.

Profiles also help search. People search for lawyers by name, and a well-structured profile page with person schema gives the firm, not a directory, the top result for that name.

Professional rules

Bar and law society rules govern how firms advertise, including claims about results and specialisms. We keep copy factual and ask the firm to approve all claims before launch.
