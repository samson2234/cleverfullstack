The problem

Translation is only half the job

A line can be translated correctly and still fail in the game. It overflows the dialogue box, a variable lands in the wrong place, the joke falls flat, or a character who sounded sharp in Japanese sounds flat in English. Players notice, reviews notice, and the fix after launch costs far more than doing it properly the first time.

We translate with the finished screen in mind. Every project starts with a glossary and a character voice guide, every string is checked against its context, and where you can give us a build we play it and log every overflow, truncation and context error before your players ever see it.

Why direct Japanese, English and Thai matters

When a Japanese game reaches Thai players through English, it has been translated twice: Japanese to English, then English to Thai. Every pass loses something, from a character's way of speaking to a pun that only works in the original. We translate Japanese to Thai directly, and we review existing Japanese to Thai pipelines for live-service games, so the Thai version is built from the source, not from a copy of a copy.

The same care goes the other way. Western games entering Japan need more than correct Japanese: they need the right level of politeness for each character, names that read naturally in katakana, and UI text that fits the screen. We plan that before the first string is translated.

Your text, inside the game, before your players see it

Most localization problems only show up on screen: a line that is too long for the box, a {player_name} tag in the wrong place, Thai or Japanese text cut off by a font that does not support it. Our LQA is done inside the build, and every issue is logged with a screenshot, the string ID and a suggested fix.

Because CleverStack is also a full-stack web development studio, we understand string files, variables and how a UI handles different lengths of text. When a problem is in the code rather than the translation, we can say exactly where it is.

How we work

From first file to final build, in four steps

1. Brief, NDA and test

Send the files, word count, target languages and deadline. We sign your NDA first, and a short translation test is available so you can judge the quality before you commit.

2. Quote, glossary and style guide

A written quote per word or per project, then a shared glossary and character voice guide, so names, terms and tone stay the same across every file and every update.

3. Translate and adapt

Translation and cultural adaptation in your CAT tool or file format, with questions batched and sent early so nothing is guessed.

4. Review, LQA and delivery

A second-pass review, in-build checks where you give us a build, and delivery in the original format, ready to import. We stay on hand for patches and live-ops updates.

Common questions

Translation & Localization Services: answered

Which languages do you translate?+

Japanese, English and Thai. Our main pairs are Japanese to English, English to Japanese, Thai to and from English, and Japanese to and from Thai directly, without translating through English first.

How much does game translation cost?+

We quote per word or per project, depending on the type of text, the deadline and whether you need LQA, subtitles or lettering-ready scripts. You get a written quote before any work starts, with no hidden fees.

How fast can you translate?+

One linguist translates about 2,500 words per day at full quality. For larger releases we split the work across vetted linguists who follow the same glossary and style guide, so the voice stays consistent.

Can we test your quality first?+

Yes. We are happy to complete a short translation test on your own text, and samples of past work are available under NDA.

Do you sign NDAs?+

Yes, always before we receive any unreleased material. We never name a client or a title in public without written permission.

What file formats and tools do you work with?+

memoQ, Trados, Phrase, Gridly, Lokalise and Crowdin, plus JSON and PO files, spreadsheets, and Unity and Unreal localization tables. Subtitles are delivered as SRT or VTT, and manga as a script with page and panel references.

Do you do LQA testing?+

Yes. We test translated text inside pre-release PC and mobile builds and log text overflow, variable truncation, rendering and context errors in Jira, TestRail or your own tracker.

Do you translate manga and subtitles as well as games?+

Yes. We translate manga, webtoons and light novels from Japanese to English, and write subtitles for trailers, cutscenes and video content in Japanese, English and Thai.

Can you also build the game website or app?+

Yes. CleverStack is a full-stack web development studio, so the same team can build your game's website, a companion app or the tools around it, and localize them.
