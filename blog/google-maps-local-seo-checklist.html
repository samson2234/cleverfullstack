How to Show Up on Google Maps and "Near Me" Searches: A Local Business Checklist

By Henry George
← Back to all articles

In short: local results depend mostly on three things you can influence: a complete and accurate Google Business Profile, a steady flow of genuine reviews, and a website that clearly says what you do and where. Set those up properly and keep them consistent.

Someone nearby searches "plumber near me" or "dentist open now" and Google shows a map with three businesses at the top. If you are not one of them, you are mostly invisible to people who are ready to buy today. The good news is that local results are easier to influence than national ones, and most of the work is free.

Google says local results are based mainly on relevance (how well your listing matches what someone searched for), distance (how close you are to them) and prominence (how well known and trusted you are). You cannot change the distance, but you can improve the other two.

Step 1: Claim and complete your Google Business Profile

Your Google Business Profile (GBP) is the free listing that appears on Google Maps and in search. Search for your business name, or go to business.google.com, and claim it. Then fill in every field you can:

  • Primary category: choose the one that describes your main service exactly (for example "Plumber", not just "Contractor"). Add secondary categories for other services.
  • Name: use your real business name. Stuffing keywords into the name breaks Google's rules and can get your listing suspended.
  • Address and service area: if customers visit you, show your address. If you travel to them, you can hide the address and list the areas you serve.
  • Opening hours: keep them accurate, and update them for holidays.
  • Phone and website: use a number you answer, and link to the most relevant page.
  • Services and description: describe what you do in plain words, including the places you serve.
  • Photos: real photos of your premises, team and work. Add new ones regularly.
  • Booking or quote link: if available, so people can act straight away.

Step 2: Keep your details identical everywhere

Your business name, address and phone number (often called NAP) should match exactly across your website, your Google profile, Facebook, Bing, Apple Maps and any directories. Small differences ("St." versus "Street", an old phone number) confuse search engines and customers. Search your business name and fix any wrong or duplicate listings you find. Duplicates, especially, split your reviews and your credibility.

Step 3: Collect genuine reviews and reply to all of them

Reviews are the biggest visible difference between businesses in the map results, and recent, detailed reviews carry weight with customers as well as with Google. A simple routine works best:

  1. Right after a job goes well, send a message with your direct review link (your profile gives you a short link).
  2. Ask personally and briefly, and make it easy: one link, two minutes.
  3. Reply to every review, thanking people and mentioning something specific. Reply calmly and factually to criticism.

Do not buy reviews, trade them for discounts without disclosure, or write them yourself. It violates Google's policies, can get reviews removed or your profile penalised, and regulators in many countries treat fake reviews as deceptive advertising. We cover this in more depth in how to get more client reviews.

Step 4: Make your website support the listing

Google looks at your website to confirm what you do and where. Make that unmistakable:

  • Put your service and location in the page title and main heading of the relevant pages, written naturally ("Emergency plumber in Leeds").
  • Create a page for each main service, with real, useful detail rather than a thin paragraph.
  • If you serve several towns, make a genuinely different page for each (local examples, areas, customer stories). Copying one page and swapping the place name is exactly the thin content Google ignores.
  • Show your address, phone and hours in the footer and on the contact page, and embed a map.
  • Add LocalBusiness structured data so search engines can read your details reliably.
  • Make the site fast and mobile-friendly, because most local searches happen on phones. See why your website may be slow.

Step 5: Earn a few local mentions

Being mentioned by other local organisations signals that you are a real, established business. Good sources include your chamber of commerce, trade associations, suppliers and partners, local charities you support, and local news. A handful of relevant, genuine links beats dozens of spammy directory listings.

Step 6: Measure what matters

Your Google profile shows how many people called, requested directions or visited your website, and what they searched to find you. Check it monthly. Also keep a simple count of enquiries and ask new customers how they found you. Local SEO is not instant: expect it to take weeks to months to see a steady change, and be wary of anyone who promises a guaranteed number one position.

Questions we hear most

Do I need a website to appear on Google Maps?

No. A Google Business Profile can appear without one. But a website helps a lot: it gives Google more evidence about what you do, gives customers somewhere to learn more and book, and lets you rank for searches that the map listing alone cannot capture. Businesses with both usually outperform those with only one.

I work from home. Can I still have a profile?

Yes, as a service-area business. You can hide your home address and list the areas you serve instead. You must still be able to serve customers in those areas, and you must follow Google's guidelines on eligibility.

How long until I see results?

Fixing an incomplete profile and collecting a few good reviews can make a noticeable difference within a few weeks. Building a strong, lasting position in a competitive area takes months of steady effort. Treat it as a routine rather than a one-off project, and be cautious about any agency that guarantees a specific ranking.

What should I do if a competitor posts fake reviews about me?

Report them through your profile and keep records of the evidence. Reply politely and factually so other readers see your side. Do not respond with fake reviews of your own.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Using a virtual office or someone else's address as your location. Google actively removes these.
  • Creating multiple listings for the same business.
  • Putting keywords in the business name.
  • Ignoring reviews, especially negative ones.
  • Letting hours and details go out of date.
