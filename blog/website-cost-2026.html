How Much Should a Website Cost in 2026? (A Transparent Breakdown)

By Henry George
← Back to all articles

If you're a founder or business owner researching a new website, the first question is always: "How much is this going to cost me?"

The honest answer: anywhere from $0 (DIY with a template) to $50,000+ (fully custom build). But price alone tells you nothing. What matters is what you get for your money — and what the wrong choice costs you in lost revenue.

Here's a transparent breakdown of what different budgets actually buy in 2026.

The Three Tiers of Website Costs

Tier 1: DIY / Template ($0 – $500/year)

You use Wix, Squarespace, or a $59 WordPress theme. You build it yourself over a weekend. It'll look decent, load okay, and check the box. But it won't convert like a professional site, and you'll hit a wall the moment you need custom features, better SEO, or multi-language support.

Who it's for: Freelancers, side projects, micro-businesses testing an idea.

Tier 2: Professional Template ($1,500 – $5,000)

A developer takes a premium template and customizes it for your brand. You get a better design, faster load times, and basic SEO. This is the most common choice for startups and small businesses. It works — until you need to scale.

Who it's for: Established small businesses, early-stage startups with $2k–$10k/month revenue.

Tier 3: Fully Custom Build ($5,000 – $30,000+)

Every pixel, every line of code, every animation is built for your business. You get a site optimized for conversions, built for SEO, and designed to scale. It loads in under 2 seconds, ranks on Google, and converts visitors into customers. This is what we build at CleverStack.

Who it's for: Growing businesses, e-commerce stores, SaaS startups, companies running ad campaigns.

Where Your Money Actually Goes

When you pay for a custom website, here's what you're paying for:

  • Strategy & discovery (20%): Understanding your audience, your competitors, and your conversion goals.
  • Design (25%): Custom UI/UX, wireframes, prototypes — not a template reskin.
  • Development (35%): Clean, semantic code, responsive layouts, custom functionality.
  • SEO & performance (10%): Schema markup, Core Web Vitals optimization, meta strategy.
  • Testing & launch (10%): Cross-browser testing, mobile testing, speed optimization, deployment.

You're not paying for "a website." You're paying for a revenue-generating asset. That $59 template won't generate leads. A custom site, built for conversion, will pay for itself in weeks or months.

Why "Cheap" Costs More

I've seen it a dozen times: a business spends $500 on a Fiverr website. Six months later, they're paying $3,000 to fix the mess — broken mobile layouts, slow load times, bad SEO, no analytics. The cheapest option is almost always the most expensive in the long run.

Invest in quality upfront. Your website is your 24/7 sales team. Pay it like one.

A custom website is an investment, not an expense — and the ROI speaks for itself.
