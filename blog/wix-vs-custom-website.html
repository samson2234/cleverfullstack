Wix vs a Custom Website: When to Switch and What It Costs

By Henry George
← Back to all articles

In short: Wix is a good choice for getting a simple site online quickly and cheaply. A custom website makes sense when speed, search rankings, conversions, unusual features or owning your site start to matter to your growth. Switching is very doable, but the move must include redirects from every old page, or you risk losing the search traffic you have built.

Website builders such as Wix made it possible for anyone to launch a site in an afternoon. For many businesses that is exactly the right start. The question is not whether Wix is good or bad. It is whether it still fits the business you have now and the one you are building.

What Wix does well

  • Fast to launch. Pick a template, drag in your content and publish.
  • Everything in one place. Hosting, security certificates, updates and support are included in the plan.
  • Low starting cost. A monthly subscription instead of a build budget.
  • Built-in extras. Bookings, simple stores, forms and email marketing are available through Wix and its app market.

For a new business testing an idea, a personal brand or a small service business with modest needs, these are real advantages.

Signs you have outgrown it

  • The site feels slow, especially on phones. Builder sites load a lot of code you do not control. Our guide to why websites are slow explains why that matters for sales and rankings.
  • You are fighting the template. Small layout changes take hours, or the design you want simply is not possible.
  • You need features the builder cannot do well, such as a client portal, custom quote calculator, complex booking rules or connections to your CRM and other systems.
  • Search rankings have stalled despite good content, and you want full control over page structure, speed and technical SEO.
  • Monthly costs keep climbing as you add premium plans and apps.
  • You want to own your site. A Wix site runs only on Wix. You cannot export it and host it elsewhere, so leaving means rebuilding.

One or two of these may be manageable. Several together usually mean the builder is costing you more in lost enquiries than a better site would cost to build.

What a custom website changes

A custom website is designed for your business and built with modern code. In practice that means:

  • Speed. Only the code each page needs is loaded, so pages appear quickly on any phone.
  • A design shaped around conversions, not around what a template allows: clear calls to action, trust signals where they matter and short forms.
  • Full control over SEO: page structure, headings, structured data, internal links and speed.
  • Any feature you need, connected to the tools you already use.
  • Ownership. The code, domain and hosting are yours. You can move host or developer whenever you like.

You can still edit text, images and articles yourself when the site is connected to a simple editor.

Is there a middle ground?

Not every business needs to jump straight from a builder to a fully custom site. There are options in between, and the right one depends on what is holding you back.

  • Improve the Wix site you have. If the main problem is unclear messaging, weak calls to action or slow images, a focused clean-up may be enough for now. Compress images, remove apps you do not use, simplify the home page and make the contact form shorter.
  • Move to WordPress. WordPress gives you more control and ownership than a builder, with a large choice of developers, while staying easy to edit. It needs regular updates, so plan for maintenance.
  • Go custom for the pages that matter most. Some businesses start with a fast custom home page and service pages, and add the rest over time.
  • Go fully custom when you need speed, special features and full ownership together.

A good developer will tell you honestly which of these fits your goals and budget, rather than selling the biggest project. Ask what problem each option solves, how long it takes, and what it costs to run each year, not just to build.

What it costs

A Wix site costs a monthly plan, plus any premium apps, for as long as the site runs. A custom website has a one-off build cost and then a small hosting cost, which for many business sites is low. Over a few years the totals are often closer than they first appear, and the custom site is usually the one bringing in more enquiries.

How to switch without losing rankings

  1. List every page on your current site, including blog posts and product pages, and note which ones bring in search traffic.
  2. Keep or improve the content that ranks. Rewriting everything at once can cost rankings.
  3. Set up a 301 redirect from every old address to its new one, so visitors and search engines land in the right place.
  4. Move your domain so it points to the new site, keeping the same domain name.
  5. Submit the new sitemap in Google Search Console and watch for errors over the following weeks.
