How Much Does a Business Website Cost in 2026? (USA & UK Pricing Guide)

By Henry George
← Back to all articles

"How much is a website going to cost me?" It's the first question every founder asks — and the one most agencies dodge. You'll get vague ranges, hidden fees, and surprise upsells after you've already committed. I'm not going to do that.

After building sites for clients across the US, UK, and Europe, I'm laying out exactly what a business website costs in 2026. No fluff. No bait-and-switch. Just honest numbers so you can budget with confidence — whether you're a startup in Austin or an SME in Manchester.

Here's the truth: the cost depends entirely on what you need, who builds it, and how much revenue it needs to generate. A brochure site for a local plumber is a completely different project than an e-commerce platform processing thousands of orders. Let's break it all down.

1. The Real Cost Ranges: From DIY to Custom

Let's start with the numbers. In the USA, a small business website typically costs between $2,000 and $10,000. In the UK, the average lands between £1,000 and £5,000. But those ranges tell you almost nothing without context, so here's what actually determines where you fall on that spectrum:

  • DIY platforms (Wix, Squarespace, Wix): $12–$50/month. They're cheap and fast to set up, but you're locked into their templates, their speed limitations, and their branding. Fine for a side project. Risky for a business that depends on conversions.
  • Professional template customization: $2,000–$5,000. A developer tweaks an existing theme to match your brand. Faster than custom, but you're still constrained by the template's architecture.
  • Professional custom build: $5,000–$30,000+. Every page, every interaction, every line of code is designed around your business goals. This is where serious businesses land.
  • E-commerce sites: $5,000–$50,000+. Product catalogs, payment processing, inventory management, tax calculations — the complexity scales fast.
  • Web applications: $10,000–$100,000+. Custom dashboards, user authentication, APIs, databases. If it's more than a website and closer to software, expect software-level pricing.

2. The Hidden Costs Most People Forget

The build is just the beginning. Most people budget for design and development, then get blindsided by the ongoing costs that keep a website alive, secure, and performing well. Here's what you need to factor in beyond the initial build:

  • Domain name: $10–$20/year. Negligible, but don't forget to renew it.
  • Hosting: $5–$300/month depending on traffic and performance needs. Cheap shared hosting works for tiny sites; growing businesses need VPS or cloud hosting.
  • SSL certificate: Free with most hosts (Let's Encrypt), or $50–$200/year for premium options.
  • Ongoing maintenance: $50–$500/month. This covers updates, security patches, backups, and minor changes. Skip this at your own risk — an unmaintained site is a hacked site waiting to happen.
  • SEO and content: $500–$3,000/month if you're investing in organic growth. A beautiful site nobody finds is an expensive decoration.

For a typical small business in the USA, expect to spend $1,000–$5,000/year on these ongoing costs. In the UK, that translates to roughly £800–£4,000/year. Budget for it from day one, or you'll be cutting corners on maintenance within six months.

3. Why a Cheap Website Actually Costs You More

Here's a stat that should make you think: 75% of consumers judge a company's credibility based on website design alone. And 88% of users won't return after a bad experience. That's not me making numbers up — those are from Stanford research and Google's UX studies.

A $500 website from a random freelancer on Fiverr might look "fine" on day one. But if it loads slowly, looks dated on mobile, breaks on Safari, or confuses visitors about what you actually do — you're not saving money. You're losing leads. Every single day.

Think about it this way: if your website costs you $5,000 but brings in just two extra clients per month at $2,000 each, it pays for itself in under two months. A $500 site that brings in zero extra clients costs you $500. Math isn't complicated here.

Mobile-first design is non-negotiable in 2026. Over 60% of web traffic now comes from mobile devices. If your site isn't optimized for phones and tablets, you're alienating the majority of your potential customers. Responsive design isn't a premium add-on anymore — it's table stakes.

4. What Should You Actually Budget?

Rather than give you a single number, here's how to think about your budget based on your situation. If you're a local service business (plumber, lawyer, dentist) that just needs a professional presence, leads, and reviews — budget $3,000–$8,000 for the build, plus $100–$300/month for hosting and maintenance. This gets you a fast, clean, conversion-focused site that works on every device.

If you're a growing business or startup that needs custom features, integrations with your CRM, a blog for SEO, or multi-location pages — budget $8,000–$25,000 for the build. Ongoing costs land around $300–$800/month when you factor in hosting, maintenance, and basic SEO. If you're building an e-commerce store with hundreds of products, payment processing, shipping integrations, and inventory management — budget $15,000–$50,000+. The ongoing costs are higher too: $500–$2,000/month for hosting, maintenance, payment processing fees, and platform subscriptions.

The key question isn't "how cheap can I get a website?" It's "how much is a website worth to my business?" A site that generates $50k in annual revenue is worth investing $15k in. A site that generates nothing is overpriced at $0.
