WordPress vs a Custom Website for a Law Firm: Which Is Right?

By Henry George
← Back to all articles

In short: WordPress suits most small and mid-sized firms: it is affordable, easy for staff to edit and well understood by developers. A custom-built site suits firms that need top speed, minimal maintenance, a distinctive brand or features such as client portals and intake tools. Either can work well. What matters most is that the site is fast, secure, easy to update and built to turn visitors into consultations.

A law firm's website does a specific job. Most people looking for a lawyer have never hired one before. They search, compare a few firms and decide quickly who they trust enough to call. Your site has to load fast, explain clearly what you do and where, show why you can be trusted, and make it easy to get in touch.

Both WordPress and custom builds can do this. They get there differently, with different costs, risks and day-to-day experience for your team.

What WordPress is, and why so many firms use it

WordPress is free, open-source software that powers a large share of the web. A developer installs it on your hosting, adds a theme for the design and plugins for features such as forms, SEO and security. Your team then edits pages and publishes articles from a familiar dashboard.

Its strengths for a law firm are real:

  • Lower starting cost. Themes and plugins cover most features, so less is built from scratch.
  • Easy editing. Staff can update attorney profiles, practice pages and articles without a developer.
  • A large pool of developers. If your developer moves on, someone else can take over.
  • Good for content. Firms that publish legal guides and news benefit from WordPress's blogging tools.

Where WordPress needs care

Most WordPress problems come from how a site is built and looked after, not from WordPress itself.

  • Security. WordPress is a common target because it is so popular. Outdated plugins are the usual way in. A firm site needs regular updates, strong logins, backups and a security plugin or firewall. Clients trust you with sensitive matters, so a hacked site does real damage.
  • Speed. Heavy page builders and too many plugins slow sites down. A slow site loses visitors before they read a word.
  • Maintenance. Updates need to be applied and tested every month. Someone has to own that job.

Built carefully, with few plugins and a lean theme, WordPress is fast and safe. Built carelessly, it becomes slow and fragile.

What a custom website offers

A custom site is designed and coded for your firm, often with a modern framework such as Next.js, and can be connected to an easy editor for your team. Its strengths:

  • Speed. Only the code your site needs is loaded, so pages are very fast, which helps both visitors and search rankings.
  • Security. With no plugins to exploit and often no database exposed to the public, there is much less to attack.
  • Less maintenance. No monthly plugin updates to apply and test.
  • A distinctive brand. The design is not limited by what a theme allows.
  • Custom features. Client intake forms that route to the right attorney, eligibility checkers, fee calculators or a secure client portal connected to your practice management software.

The trade-offs are a higher starting cost, and changes beyond text and images usually need a developer.

What matters more than the platform

Clients do not see the platform. They see whether the site answers their questions and makes contact easy. Whichever you choose, make sure the site has:

  • A clear page for each practice area, written for clients, not other lawyers.
  • Attorney profiles with photos, experience and admissions.
  • Visible ways to get in touch on every page, with a short consultation form.
  • Trust signals such as reviews and memberships, presented in line with your jurisdiction's advertising rules.
  • Local search basics: your office address, a Google Business Profile, and pages for the areas you serve. Our local SEO checklist covers these.
  • Accessibility, so everyone can use the site, including people using screen readers.
  • Security and privacy: HTTPS, safe form handling, and a privacy policy. Our guide to website security basics explains the essentials.

Questions to ask any developer

Whichever platform you lean towards, these questions show quickly whether a developer understands law firm websites:

  • Who owns the site? The domain, hosting account and files should be in the firm's name, with your own logins.
  • Who keeps it updated and secure, how often, and what does that cost each year?
  • How are enquiries handled? Where do form submissions go, are they stored securely, and does the form avoid asking for sensitive case details up front?
  • How fast will it be on a phone? Ask for speed results from sites they have built.
  • Can my team edit it? Ask for a short demo of updating an attorney profile.

Clear, specific answers matter more than which platform the developer prefers.

How to choose

Choose WordPress if you want a solid site at a lower cost, publish articles often, and have a plan for monthly updates, either in-house or with a care plan from your developer.

Choose a custom website if speed and security are priorities, you would rather not manage plugin updates, you want a brand that stands apart, or you need features such as intake routing or a client portal.

If you already have a site, you may not need to start again. Our guide on whether to fix or rebuild helps you decide. Either way, make sure the domain, hosting and site files are in your firm's name, so you stay in control of one of your most important marketing tools.
