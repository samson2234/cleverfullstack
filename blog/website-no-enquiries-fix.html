My Website Gets Visitors but Nobody Contacts Me: 9 Fixes You Can Make This Week

By Henry George
← Back to all articles

In short: if people visit but do not contact you, the cause is usually that they cannot tell within a few seconds what you do, what to do next, or why they should trust you. All three can be fixed without a new website.

You can see people are visiting. The analytics say so. But the phone is quiet and the inbox is empty. It is tempting to think you need more traffic. In most small business sites, that is the wrong conclusion: if ten people visit and none enquire, a hundred visitors will usually give you the same result, just ten times as many people leaving.

The good news is that this is the most fixable problem a website can have. Here is how to find the cause, then nine fixes in the order we would make them.

First, make sure you are measuring the right thing

Before changing anything, work out your starting point. You need two numbers for the last 30 days: how many people visited, and how many contacted you (form submissions, calls, WhatsApp messages, bookings). Divide the second by the first. That is your enquiry rate. If you do not know either number, set up Google Analytics (it is free) and count form submissions as a goal. Also start asking every new customer one question: "How did you find us?" It is the cheapest tracking you will ever have.

Write the number down. After you make changes, you will compare against it, so you know what worked rather than guessing.

The 5-second test (do this today)

Show your homepage to three people who do not know your business. Give them five seconds, then hide it and ask:

  1. What does this company do?
  2. Who is it for?
  3. What would you do next if you wanted it?

If any of them hesitates, you have found the problem. Almost every fix below exists to make those three answers obvious.

Fix 1: Say what you do and who it is for, in the first line

Many sites open with "Welcome to our website" or a vague slogan. Visitors do not stay to decode it. Replace the main headline with one plain sentence that names the service and the customer.

  • Vague: "Excellence in every detail."
  • Clear: "Emergency plumbing for homes in Leeds, usually within 2 hours."

Use the words your customers would type into Google, not the words you use inside the business.

Fix 2: Give each page one main action, and show it early

If your page asks people to call, email, download, subscribe, follow you and read the blog, most will do none of them. Pick the one action you want most (usually "request a quote" or "book a call"), make that button the most obvious thing on the screen, and repeat it at natural stopping points down the page. On a phone, check that the button is visible without scrolling.

Fix 3: Make contacting you effortless on a phone

Most visitors will be on a phone. A phone number written as plain text forces them to copy and paste. Make it a tap-to-call link. If your customers use WhatsApp (very common outside the US), add a WhatsApp button. Put the options in the header, not buried on a contact page.

Fix 4: Shorten your form

Every extra field loses people. For a first enquiry, name, email and a message box are enough. Ask for budget, address and company size later, in the conversation. If you need a phone number, mark it optional and say why ("only if you prefer a call"). Test the form yourself on your phone: does it autofill, is it easy to tap, does it confirm clearly that it sent?

Fix 5: Prove you are real and good

People are cautious about paying a business they have never met. Show evidence near the main button: genuine reviews with a name and role, photos of your real work or team, recognisable clients (with permission), certifications, and years in business if you can state them honestly. One specific, named review is worth more than a page of claims. Never invent or exaggerate proof; it backfires, and in many countries it is also against advertising rules.

Fix 6: Be upfront about price

Visitors who cannot tell whether you are affordable often leave to avoid an awkward call. You do not have to publish a full price list. A "from" price, a typical range, or a short "how pricing works" section keeps the right people and filters out the wrong ones, which means better enquiries, not just more.

Fix 7: Set expectations and reply quickly

Under the form, say what happens next: "We reply within one business day." Send an automatic confirmation email so the person knows the message arrived. Then actually reply fast. A prospect who contacts three businesses usually goes with the first helpful answer. If you cannot answer quickly yourself, an auto-reply plus a booking link buys you time.

Fix 8: Check it on a real phone, on mobile data

Do not rely on a resized desktop browser. Pick up a phone, switch off Wi-Fi, and try to do what a customer would do. Is the text readable without zooming? Do pop-ups cover the screen? Does anything jump around as it loads? Does the page load in a few seconds? If you are not sure about speed, our guide on finding and fixing a slow website walks through it.

Fix 9: Remove what gets in the way

Auto-playing sliders, full-screen pop-ups that appear the moment someone arrives, music, long intro animations and cluttered menus all add friction. Ask of every element: does this help someone contact me? If not, remove it or move it lower down.

How to know it worked

Change one or two things at a time and give each change a couple of weeks, longer if you have little traffic. Then recalculate your enquiry rate. Improvement does not need to be dramatic to be valuable: if you get twice as many enquiries from the same visitors, your marketing just became twice as efficient without spending more. If you want to see what that could mean in money for your own numbers, try the calculator on our homepage. It uses only the figures you enter.

When it is time to get help

If you have worked through these and enquiries are still low, the cause may be deeper: the wrong audience, a slow site, a confusing structure, or a design that no longer reflects your business. That is when a proper audit is worth it. Whether the answer is a few targeted fixes or a rebuild, you should hear it plainly and with a fixed price.
